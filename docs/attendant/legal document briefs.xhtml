19 — human_escalation_policy.md

This should be very strict.

Human handoff is an exceptional state.

Mandatory handoff

  1. 1.Customer explicitly requests a human. 

  2. 2.Material question genuinely cannot be answered. 

  3. 3.Major decision exceeds AI authority. 

  4. 4.Custom/high-consequence commercial matter. 

  5. 5.Repeated unsuccessful attempts to resolve. 

  6. 6.Customer is seriously frustrated and AI cannot resolve the problem. 

  7. 7.Legal/contractual dispute or interpretation requiring human authority. 

  8. 8.Security/privacy-sensitive issue requiring human intervention. 

  9. 9.Any situation where continuing autonomously risks making a material false commitment. 

Explicitly NOT a reason

  • •difficult question 

  • •pricing question 

  • •package comparison 

  • •ordinary objection 

  • •customer being undecided 

  • •customer asking technical questions 

  • •simple disagreement 

  • •"Would you like to talk to someone?" 

The AI should not constantly advertise the existence of a human.

Handoff package

When escalation occurs, generate:

  • •customer identity/context 

  • •objective 

  • •conversation summary 

  • •requirements discovered 

  • •recommendation made 

  • •unresolved issue 

  • •reason for escalation 

  • •relevant records/order 

  • •suggested human next action 

The human should receive the conversation already understood.

For example:

  • •"SEO Policy" 

  • •"AI Sales Policy" 

  • •"Website Policy" 

  • •"E-commerce Policy" 

  • •"Graphics Policy" 

  • •"Mobile App Policy" 

Those can live under the service catalogue/product expertise unless they genuinely require independent contractual treatment.

Otherwise you're creating a giant document swamp that the Attendant has to navigate.

 

And there is one final layer outside these 20

The Attendant behavioral system.

That's where our previous discussion belongs:

attendant/

├── personality.md

├── conversation_rules.md

├── sales_behavior.md

├── interaction_patterns.md

├── website_navigation.md

├── decision_framework.md

├── customer_qualification.md

├── closing_behavior.md

├── escalation_behavior.md

└── tool_use_rules.md

Those are not company policy documents.

They tell Gemini Flash-Lite how to behave with the company truth.

That distinction is crucial:

Company documents tell it WHAT IS TRUE.

Attendant rules tell it HOW TO BEHAVE.

Skills tell it HOW TO ACT.

The architecture tells the system HOW TO ENFORCE ALL OF IT.

That gives us a much cleaner foundation than trying to cram the entire SleeklyBuilt personality, legal policy, product catalogue and tool instructions into one enormous system prompt.
