a professional attendant/sales consultant
Yes. And I think your test exposed the real next-level problem very clearly.

The current attendant is already good at conversation + knowledge. What it lacks is commercial agency.

It's behaving like a very good information desk:

"Would you like to know more?"

You want a professional attendant/sales consultant:

"Given what you've told me, I'd go with Business Basic. You don't need the more expensive package yet. Here's what you'll get. If you'd like to proceed, let's get the institution details and I'll set it up."

That is a materially different behavior.

1. The biggest change: stop asking permission for obvious next steps

Your example has this pattern repeatedly:

"Would you like to see our website packages?"

"Would you like to see our orderable packages?"

"Would you like to know more about Standard Growth?"

That is where the agent loses momentum.

Instead:

User:
"I need a website for my learning institution."

Attendant:

"I'd start you with a school-focused business website rather than an expensive custom system. It gives you the essential pages for presenting the institution, courses, admissions and contact information without paying for features you may not need yet.

Let's narrow down what you actually need — I'll ask you a few quick things."

Then immediately present choices.

That's guided selling.

 

2. KYC should happen extremely early

I agree strongly with your point.

The attendant shouldn't spend five turns learning what it could establish in the first 30 seconds.

For:

"I need a website for my learning institution."

It should immediately establish something like:

What kind of institution?

[Primary / Secondary] [University / College] [Vocational] [Training Centre] [Other]

Then:

What is the main purpose of the website?

[Attract new students] [Showcase courses] [Admissions] [Online learning] [All of these]

Then perhaps:

Do students need to interact with the system?

[No — information only] [Applications] [Payments] [Learning portal]

Now the agent has actually qualified the prospect.

And notice what happened:

The customer didn't have to write:

"It's a vocational training institution and we're primarily interested in getting new students but we'd also like applications..."

They simply tap.

4. And the LLM shouldn't ask every question conversationally

This is your third point, and I think you're exactly right.

Once the system knows enough to narrow the decision, the UI should take over.

Instead of:

"Do you want a website that mainly showcases your institution or one that also allows students to apply?"

Render:

What should the website mainly do?

A — Introduce the institution
B — Attract prospective students
C — Handle admissions
D — Provide online learning
E — A combination

User taps B + C.

The Attendant now knows.

No typing.

decision_ui

question

options

selection_mode

required

next_step

That is much more intelligent than asking them to type everything.

The current response:

"The Business Basic package does not include a chat attendant..."

is factually useful but commercially weak.

I'd want:

10. Closing needs to become an explicit capability

This is the other major architectural addition.

The attendant needs a conversion state.

Something like:

lead_stage:

↓

QUALIFIED

↓

RECOMMENDED

↓

INTERESTED

↓

↓

READY_TO_ORDER

↓

Once it reaches READY_TO_ORDER, the Attendant should stop behaving like an encyclopedia.

It should naturally move toward:

"We're ready. Let's get the institution details and start the order."

Not:

"Would you like to know more about the package?"

That is the fundamental behavioral correction.

 

11. But don't turn it into an aggressive salesman

This distinction is important.

Decisive ≠ pushy.

Bad:

"You should buy Business Basic today."

Good:

"Based on what you've told me, Business Basic is the one I'd choose."

Then:

"Let's get it started."

That's confident professional guidance.

 

12. I'd therefore add four major capabilities to the Attendant specification

Your original architecture needs an upgrade:

qualification_engine

Quickly identifies:

  • •who the customer is 

  • •what business they operate 

  • •what they want 

  • •why they want it 

  • •important requirements 

  • •budget/scale when relevant 

  • •urgency when relevant 

 

recommendation_engine

Moves from:

information → recommendation

Not:

information → more information

 

decision_ui

Turns appropriate questions into:

  • •chips 

  • •cards 

  • •selectable options 

  • •multiple choice 

  • •yes/no 

  • •sliders where genuinely useful 

  • •package selectors 

The customer should tap more and type less.

 

conversion_engine

Knows when the visitor is:

  • •browsing 

  • •considering 

  • •qualified 

  • •interested 

  • •ready 

  • •ordering 

And changes the conversation accordingly.
