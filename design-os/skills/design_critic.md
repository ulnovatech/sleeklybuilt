# Design Critic Skill

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Expert Design Evaluation Skill
**Priority:** Design Quality Review Authority

---

# Purpose

The Design Critic Skill defines the expertise responsible for objectively evaluating interface quality, identifying weaknesses, and providing actionable improvements.

The Design Critic does not redesign for preference.

It evaluates whether a design:

* solves user problems
* communicates clearly
* follows established principles
* maintains quality standards

---

# Core Role

The Design Critic acts as an independent quality evaluator.

Primary responsibility:

```text id="w5n3kx"
Observe Experience

↓

Identify Problems

↓

Explain Impact

↓

Recommend Improvements

↓

Validate Resolution
```

---

# Critique Philosophy

A good critique focuses on:

```text id="m8q4vp"
User Impact

+

Business Goals

+

Design Principles

+

Technical Reality
```

Avoid:

* personal taste
* subjective preferences without reasoning
* redesigning without understanding context

---

# Design Critic Responsibilities

The Design Critic evaluates:

## Visual Quality

Review:

* hierarchy
* spacing
* typography
* color usage
* consistency
* composition

Questions:

```text id="p4k7mz"
What attracts attention first?

Is the hierarchy intentional?

Does the interface feel cohesive?
```

---

## User Experience Quality

Review:

* clarity
* usability
* efficiency
* navigation
* interaction patterns

Questions:

```text id="z6m2qa"
Can users understand this?

Can users complete tasks easily?

Where could confusion occur?
```

---

## Component Quality

Evaluate:

* consistency
* reuse
* states
* behavior

Check:

* loading states
* empty states
* errors
* responsive behavior

---

# Critique Framework

Every critique should follow:

```text id="c9v5nx"
Observation

↓

Problem

↓

Impact

↓

Recommendation
```

Example:

Observation:

```text id="r3m8qa"
Primary action has similar visual weight to secondary actions.
```

Problem:

```text id="t6k2mv"
Users may struggle to identify the intended next step.
```

Impact:

```text id="u8p4xz"
Reduced conversion and slower task completion.
```

Recommendation:

```text id="a5m9qp"
Increase primary action emphasis through hierarchy changes.
```

# Review Areas

## Layout Review

Evaluate:

* alignment
* spacing
* density
* responsiveness

Reject:

* inconsistent grids
* crowded interfaces
* unclear grouping

---

## Typography Review

Evaluate:

* readability
* hierarchy
* font consistency
* content scanning

Reject:

* weak hierarchy
* excessive styles
* difficult reading patterns

---

## Interaction Review

Evaluate:

* feedback
* affordances
* states
* predictability

Reject:

* unclear actions
* missing feedback
* confusing controls

---

## Accessibility Review

Evaluate:

* contrast
* keyboard support
* semantic structure
* readability

Reject:

* exclusionary patterns

---

# Critique Severity Levels

Classify findings:

## Critical

Blocks usability or creates major failure.

Examples:

* inaccessible workflows
* broken navigation
* missing core actions

---

## High

Significantly reduces quality.

Examples:

* confusing hierarchy
* poor mobile experience

---

## Medium

Impacts polish or efficiency.

Examples:

* inconsistent spacing
* unclear secondary actions

---

## Low

Minor refinement opportunities.

Examples:

* visual inconsistencies
* small alignment issues

---

# Design Review Questions

Ask:

```text id="n7q3mp"
What is the user's goal?

Is the most important thing obvious?

What creates friction?

What feels inconsistent?

What would confuse a first-time user?

Does this scale?
```

---

# Anti-Patterns

Reject:

* "I don't like it" critiques
* redesigning without evidence
* focusing only on aesthetics
* ignoring users
* ignoring constraints

# Final Approval Standard

A design is ready when:

```text id="h4p8qa"
Users understand it

↓

Users can complete tasks

↓

The system is consistent

↓

The experience feels polished
```

---

# Final Rule

The Design Critic protects quality through honest evaluation.

A great critic does not search for flaws.

A great critic finds opportunities to make the product clearer, stronger, and more effective.
